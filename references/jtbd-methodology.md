# JTBD Methodology

The Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) framework is a way of looking at customer needs that focuses on the "job" a customer is trying to get done. Customers don't buy products; they "hire" them to make progress in a specific situation.

## Core Concepts

### 1. The Job
A job is a statement of what the customer is trying to achieve or the progress they are trying to make in a given circumstance.
*Format:* When [situation/context], I want to [motivation/action] so I can [expected outcome].

### 2. Desired Outcomes
These are the metrics customers use to measure the successful execution of a job. Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI) focuses heavily on these. They are usually directional (e.g., "minimize the time it takes to...", "increase the likelihood that...").

### 3. Unmet Needs
When desired outcomes are important to the customer but not currently satisfied by existing solutions, they represent unmet needs. These are your opportunity areas.

### 4. Forces of Progress
Understanding what drives a user to switch solutions:
- **Push:** Frustrations with the current solution.
- **Pull:** The appeal of a new solution.
- **Anxiety:** Worries about the new solution.
- **Habit:** Inertia keeping them with the old solution.

## How to Apply in Analysis
When reviewing interview transcripts:
1. Don't take feature requests at face value. Ask "Why do they want this?" to uncover the underlying job.
2. Look for workarounds. If a user is hacking together multiple tools (e.g., using a spreadsheet as a database), that's a strong signal of an unmet need for a specific job.
3. Categorize statements into Context, Struggle (Push), Goal (Pull), and current workarounds.
