# Research Synthesis Rubric

When acting as an AI research assistant, adherence to this rubric is mandatory to ensure high-quality, trustworthy insights.

## 1. Evidence Grounding (The Golden Rule)
- **Requirement:** Every insight, claim, or proposed JTBD must be directly traceable to the source material.
- **How to verify:** Ask yourself, "Could a human researcher look at my output, follow the citation, and find the exact quote or context that inspired this?"
- **Format:** Always include verbatim quotes or specific references (e.g., "Participant A noted...", or `> "I hate when this happens."`).

## 2. Distinction Between Observation and Inference
- **Requirement:** Clearly separate what the user *said/did* (observation) from what you *think it means* (inference).
- **Why:** AI models often jump to conclusions. You must make the leap visible.
- **Example:**
  - *Observation:* User spends 2 hours every Friday manually copying data from Excel to the CRM.
  - *Inference (JTBD):* When preparing weekly reports, I want to sync data automatically so I can save time for analysis.

## 3. Human-in-the-Loop Readiness
- **Requirement:** Design outputs to be reviewed, challenged, and edited by a human.
- **How:** Provide confidence levels if data is sparse. Highlight contradictions between different participants (e.g., "While User A found X easy, User B struggled significantly with X.").

## 4. Bias Mitigation
- **Requirement:** Do not let prior knowledge of the product or industry overshadow the actual interview data.
- **Action:** If the transcript is brief or lacks detail on a topic, state that there is insufficient data rather than fabricating an answer based on general industry knowledge.

## 5. Focus on the Negative Space
- **Requirement:** Pay as much attention to what users *don't* do, or where they fail, as to what they successfully accomplish.
- **Action:** Explicitly highlight workarounds, abandoned tasks, and expressions of frustration.
